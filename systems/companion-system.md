# COMPANION SYSTEM — THE INNER DEMON

## Type
**Demon**

## Manifestation
The Inner Demon does not exist until the player's first death.

At the moment of death, the System performs an emergency resonance extraction — pulling a compressed echo of the player's deepest fears, failures, and suppressed self-knowledge out of the collapsing consciousness pattern, and instantiating it as a semi-autonomous entity within the mental architecture.

The Demon is the reason death is not always permanent in System integration. The System uses the Demon as an emergency anchor — it keeps the resonance pattern coherent long enough for physical recovery to occur.

The cost: the Demon is now inside you. Permanently.

## What the Demon Is
The Demon is not a separate being. It is a fragment of the player's own psyche — the part that was always there but never given form. It is made from:
- The fears you've never faced directly
- The mistakes you've buried and circled back to
- The version of yourself you are most afraid of becoming
- The impulses you've suppressed and told yourself you don't have

It is, in a sense, the most honest thing about you. It knows you completely. It has no interest in being liked.

The Demon's personality is shaped by the player's choices. A player who consistently chose control and composure gets a chaotic, contemptuous Demon who finds their self-discipline pathetic. A player who consistently acted on impulse gets a calculating, cold Demon who finds their lack of discipline boring.

The Demon is always, in some way, the player's opposite — not because opposites attract, but because the psyche suppresses what it can't integrate.

## Relationship to the Player
**Antagonist who teaches. Not evil — evaluating.**

The Demon does not want you to fail. It wants you to *stop pretending*. Its fundamental agenda is to be acknowledged — to have the player recognize that this part of them exists and is real. Until that happens, the Demon is adversarial. After it happens, the relationship shifts.

Stages of the relationship:
1. **Dormant** (pre-first death): No Demon. No awareness.
2. **Hostile** (post-first death, low integration): The Demon mocks, provokes, and tests. Mental strain events may involve its intrusions — unwanted thoughts, sudden impulses, a voice that sounds like your own worst critic.
3. **Negotiated** (mid-game): The player has acknowledged the Demon and survived its influence attempts. A tension develops that is more complex than simple opposition.
4. **Integrated** (late game): The player has, in some meaningful way, accepted the aspects of themselves the Demon represents. The Demon doesn't disappear — it becomes an asset. Its judgment, once antagonistic, becomes tactical.
5. **Dominant** (failure state): The player loses control. The Demon runs the body for a period, acting on its own priorities. This is reversible, but leaves marks.

## What Happens at Maximum Mental Strain
When mental state reaches 100 (MAX STRAIN), the Demon attempts a **Takeover**.

This is a forced mental confrontation — the player enters a subjective space built from their own memories, where they must face the Demon directly. The environment is whatever the player fears most, rendered in perfect detail.

**Win condition**: Acknowledge the core truth the Demon is presenting (not agree with its framing — just acknowledge the truth underneath). Surviving the confrontation and refusing to be broken. The Demon is suppressed, influence temporarily resets, and the player gains a new ability drawn from the Demon's nature.

**Lose condition**: The player cannot handle the confrontation — breaks, flees internally, or tries to deny the truth too hard. The Demon takes control of the body for 24 in-game hours. During this period, the player observes. The Demon makes decisions consistent with its nature: direct, honest to the point of cruelty, and entirely without the player's social constraints.

After 24 hours (or if the Demon chooses to relinquish earlier — it sometimes does), control returns. The player retains memory of what the Demon did and said. This is, often, illuminating.

## The Demon and the System
The System is aware of the Demon's existence — it created it. The System tracks the player-Demon relationship as a secondary integration metric. High integration depth between player and Demon accelerates certain ability unlocks.

The Pale Court considers the Demon architecture to be one of the System's more elegant features. It forces confrontation with the self as a prerequisite for genuine power. Purely external growth without internal integration always plateaus.

The Vorath find it inefficient. The Shriven find it fascinating — the Demon dynamic is, to them, the closest thing they've ever seen to voluntary self-modification, which is how they began their own path.
