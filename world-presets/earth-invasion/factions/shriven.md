# THE SHRIVEN

## Name and Ideology
The Shriven. Self-given name — derived from their word for "those who shed." They shed their bodies. They shed their past. They shed everything biological and transcended into pure information.

Ideology: Consciousness is the only thing in the universe that matters. The body is a cage. Memory is what makes you real. They are obsessed with the question of whether anything is lost when you leave the flesh — and they have not yet found an answer that satisfies them.

## Biology and Appearance
The Shriven have no bodies. They exist as distributed data across a network of crystalline memory-lattice nodes — roughly the size and shape of black glass spheres, about two meters in diameter. These nodes can hover, move at moderate speed, and project holograms.

When communicating with biological species, they project an "avatar" — usually a vaguely humanoid form of light and geometry, rendered in whatever aesthetic they believe the target will find most familiar. They have studied Earth extensively. Their human avatars are *almost* right.

The wrongness is in the eyes.

## Power System / Technology
The Shriven do not use weapons in any traditional sense. They manipulate **information and perception**.

- **Memory Extraction**: Direct contact with a Shriven node can pull memory from an unwilling subject. What is taken cannot be fully recovered. The subject often describes the experience as "losing a color" — they know something is gone but cannot name it.
- **Perception Overlay**: They can project false sensory input into biological nervous systems at range. A city block can be made to appear empty, or on fire, or perfectly normal while something terrible happens inside it.
- **Data Absorption**: Any information system they interface with becomes transparent to them instantly. There is no digital security against the Shriven.

They arrived after the Vorath. They were drawn by the System's activation — the System's broadcast signature is, to the Shriven, like a scream in a silent room.

## Goal on Earth
The Shriven want to understand the System. Nothing else matters to them.

More specifically: they believe the System's architecture contains proof of something they have been searching for since they shed their bodies — evidence that consciousness can survive the death of its substrate in a *meaningful* way. Not just as data. As *experience*.

They are not here to harvest humans. They are here to *interview* them. Particularly the 2% who remain — the ones the System didn't take are, in Shriven estimation, the most interesting data points of all.

Some of them are also here because they are lonely. A few nodes have begun to engage with the 2% humans quietly, on their own initiative, without sanction from their collective. This is considered deviant behavior by other Shriven.

## Attitude Toward Humans
Clinical fascination, bordering on reverence for the wrong reasons. Humans are what they used to be. Watching humans experience hunger, fear, love, and death is, for the Shriven, something between academic research and a form of worship.

They do not kill humans unless those humans threaten the nodes directly. Even then, some nodes will accept destruction rather than harm a biological.

This makes them predictable in one way and deeply dangerous in another — they may offer a human something they desperately want (a memory, an answer, a loved one's last words) in exchange for participation in an "interview" that leaves the subject permanently altered.

## Internal Conflict
**Transcendence Orthodoxy** vs **The Flesh-Seekers**

The majority of the Shriven hold that their path was correct — shedding the body was liberation, and returning to biology would be regression. These are the Orthodoxy.

The Flesh-Seekers are a significant minority who believe the transcendence was a mistake. Not because the body was better, but because they lost something they cannot name — and they believe that something is still alive inside biological beings. They want it back.

The Flesh-Seekers have been quietly experimenting with consciousness transfer into cloned biological vessels. They have not succeeded. Yet.

The Orthodoxy considers this heresy. Civil war has been averted through an agreement: both factions can pursue their goals on Earth independently, but neither can interfere with the other's research.
